Build it yourself

Short answer

The fastest way to make this stick is to build a small version of each stage and compare your numbers with the ones in this course.

TopicHands-on projectDone whenStart with
Train a tiny GPTTrain a character-level transformer on a small corpus on a laptop or one cloud GPU; watch the loss fall and samples improve.You can report the config, training time, train and held-out loss curves, and a few samples, and explain what changed as loss fell.nanoGPT46
Data and tokenizersDeduplicate a slice of web text with MinHash, then train a BPE tokenizer and measure characters per token across languages.You report how many near-duplicates you found at your threshold and characters per token for at least three languages, with a sentence on why they differ.FineWeb write-up3
Scaling on a budgetTrain three model sizes at fixed compute and plot your own small iso-FLOP curve.You report each model's size, tokens, FLOPs and held-out loss, and interpret the trend, even if your sizes don't bracket the optimum or noise hides it.Chinchilla8, Smol Training Playbook1
Multi-GPU trainingRent two GPUs for an hour; run the same job with data parallel, then FSDP; record memory and step time.A table of peak memory per GPU and step time for both setups, with the config and an explanation of the difference.Ultra-Scale Playbook2
Post-trainingFine-tune a small open model with SFT, then DPO on a preference dataset; compare answers before and after.The same ten prompts answered by the base, SFT and DPO models side by side, with the datasets and settings, and your notes on what changed.Hugging Face TRL49, DPO paper18
ServingServe a small model with vLLM; measure time to first token and throughput as you raise concurrency, and compare with chapter 6's numbers.A chart of time to first token and tokens per second at each concurrency level, with the model, GPU and prompt lengths, and your reading of where it saturates.vLLM54
Build a harnessWrite a 200-line agent loop with two tools (read file, run tests) against a model API; add compaction and a budget limit.A log of every model call and tool call for a planted bug, the total tokens used against your budget, and whether the tests passed.Building effective agents40, MCP42
Infrastructure watchTrack one new cluster announcement per month: chips, power, network, cooling. Plug it into chapter 14's power calculator.A dated table of at least three clusters with sources, and your power estimate next to the announced figure with the assumptions you used.Epoch AI data48

How to keep learning

Keep a short journal per chapter: the one formula that matters, one number you computed yourself, and one question you couldn't answer. Revisit the datacenter chapter every few months, since that's the part of this field changing fastest. Whenever a lab publishes a technical report or postmortem, read it against these chapters; most of it will map onto something you've already played with.
