PART 2 · CHAPTER 9Rewind · compute budget

How big, how long, how much?

Short answer

Training cost is roughly 6 × parameters × training tokens operations. Teams split a fixed budget between a bigger model and more data, guided by "scaling laws" fitted on small runs, and then pay for months of thousands of chips plus all the experiments around them.

Scaling laws

Bigger brains that read more books get smarter, in a surprisingly predictable way. That lets builders guess how good a giant model will be by testing small ones first.

Researchers noticed that loss falls smoothly as you add parameters, data and compute, following simple curves. With a fixed budget, there's a best balance: a model too big for its data is under-trained; a model too small can't absorb all that data.

OpenAI's 2020 study fitted power laws to loss7; DeepMind's Chinchilla (2022) showed that parameters and tokens should grow roughly in proportion; its results imply around 20 tokens per parameter8. In practice teams overtrain small models far past that point because serving cost depends on model size: Llama 3 8B saw about 15T tokens (close to 1,900 per parameter) and SmolLM3 saw 11T at 3B91. Mixture-of-experts models change the balance again, since compute follows active parameters.

L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β , C ≈ 6ND

Minimising L subject to C = 6ND gives N_opt ∝ C^{β/(α+β)} and D_opt ∝ C^{α/(α+β)}. With α ≈ β the two grow as √C each, which is the "scale both together" result.

The hidden bill

Before baking the big cake, bakers make lots of tiny test cakes. Those cost flour too.

The final training run is only part of the cost. Teams run many smaller experiments to choose the architecture and data, and they lose time to hardware failures and bugs.

Hugging Face's SmolLM3 accounting is unusually open: about 276k H100-hours for the main run, plus 161k more for ablations and recovering from a problem mid-run, over half as much again1. DeepSeek's widely quoted $5.6M for V3 covers only the official training stages (2.788M GPU-hours at an assumed $2/hour), and the report says it excludes prior research and ablation experiments10. Add staff, data, evaluation and the cluster itself, and the full cost of a frontier model is far higher.

Check yourself

Why do teams train small models on far more tokens than "compute-optimal"?

Chinchilla minimises training cost alone. Including inference cost favours smaller, longer-trained models.
