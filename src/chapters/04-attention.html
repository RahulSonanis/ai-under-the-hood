How does it understand what "it" refers to?

"The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired." You know at once that "it" is the animal. Change one word, "too wide", and "it" becomes the street. This chapter shows the mechanism that lets a model make that switch, and where in the sentence it happens.

What's going on

Inside a model, every word is a list of numbers, a vector. On its own, the vector for "it" knows only that it is a pronoun. Attention is how a word gathers what it needs from the words before it. Each word makes a query (what am I looking for?) and every word offers a key (what do I contain?). The better a key matches the query, the more of that word's information, its value, flows in.

Attention only looks back. A model writes left to right, so when it is at "it" the last word doesn't exist yet. That's why "it" splits its attention, ? to the animal and ? to the street. The question is settled at the deciding word: "?" sends ? to the animal and ? to the street. When you change that word, nothing before it moves.

A model runs many of these attention heads side by side, each looking for something different: one follows meaning, one just looks one word back, one parks on the first word when there's nothing to fetch. They sit inside a stack of layers. In each layer, attention adds what it gathered to the word's vector, then a small network called the MLP works on the result. How it works queries, keys, values and the stack

Your toy model. Every number in the simulation is computed, not drawn by hand, but the model is tiny and its weights are set by hand. Each word has a few labelled numbers (is it a living thing, a place, a pronoun, a word that describes a living thing or a place). Real models learn thousands of numbers per word from data, and most of them have no simple label. Researchers have still found heads in trained models that track the previous word, heads that link a word to what it refers to, and heads that dump spare attention on the first token, an "attention sink"61. The tired/wide sentence pair is the example Google used to introduce the Transformer, the design these models use57. That first Transformer read in both directions; a model that writes left to right can only make the link at the deciding word.

Queries, keys and values. Each head multiplies a word's vector by three learned grids of numbers (weight matrices) to get its query, key and value. It scores the word's query against the key of every word up to and including itself, scales the scores, turns them into weights that add to 100% with softmax, and adds up the values with those weights5. Blocking every later word is called the causal mask. The previous-word head in the simulation finds its target the way real models usually encode position: queries and keys are rotated by an angle that grows with position (a method called RoPE), so the score depends on how far apart two words are.

The stack. A model like Meta's open Llama 3 8B, an 8-billion-parameter model, has 32 layers, each with 32 attention heads, and every word carries 4,096 numbers9. Each layer adds its results onto the word's vector rather than replacing it, so information builds up as it flows upward; this running vector is often called the residual stream. After the last layer, the final word's vector is turned into the next-token probabilities you met in chapter 3.

The cost. Every word scores every earlier word, so the work grows with the square of the length: double the text, four times the scores. The keys and values of earlier words are exactly the notes a server keeps in memory while it writes (chapter 7), and tricks like FlashAttention compute attention in small tiles to avoid slow memory traffic44. For a picture-by-picture walkthrough, Jay Alammar's illustrated guide is a good next read47.

The maths one attention weight, step by step
In words

Multiply the query and a key number by number and add the products (the dot product). Divide by the square root of how many numbers each vector has. Do this for every word up to this one, then softmax: raise e to each score and divide by the total, so the weights add to 100%. The word's new information is the weighted mix of the values.
