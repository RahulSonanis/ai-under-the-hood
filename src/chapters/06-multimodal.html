Chapter 6 · How a model answers

How can a chatbot see a photo?

The model you've met so far only reads tokens. Yet you can show it a photo of a pill bottle and ask what the label says, or send it a voice note. The trick is to turn pictures and sound into tokens too. In this chapter you do the cutting, and pay for it.

About 13 minutes · one simulation · you'll learn how a picture becomes patches, why sharper pictures cost far more tokens, and how sound joins in the same way.
From the last chapter

A reasoning model “thinks” before it answers. What does that cost?

Thinking is the model writing extra tokens you don't see. More thinking usually buys accuracy, paid for with waiting time and the price of those tokens.

What's going on

A language model reads a list of vectors (lists of numbers), one per token (chapter 2 showed how each word piece becomes a row of numbers). To read a picture, the model needs the picture as vectors too. So the picture is cut into a grid of small squares, the patches, and each patch is turned into one vector the same size as a word's. From then on the model reads patches exactly like words, after the text of your question.

Your picture is 224×224 pixels cut into 16-pixel patches: 14 across and 14 down, so 196 tokens, about as long as 147 words of text. Doubling the width and height gives four times the patches. That is the trade-off you played with: a sharper picture keeps small text readable (your label's letters are about – tall) but costs many more tokens, and tokens are what you pay for and what fills the model's memory.

Sound works the same way. A voice note is first drawn as a spectrogram, a picture of which pitches are loud at each moment. That picture is cut into thin time slices, one token for every 20 milliseconds, so 4 seconds of speech becomes 200 tokens.

How it works patches, encoders, adapters, sound

Patches. The idea comes from the Vision Transformer, a 2020 model from Google whose paper was titled “An Image is Worth 16x16 Words”. It cut a 224×224 image into 16×16-pixel patches, flattened each patch into a list of its 768 colour values (16 × 16 × 3), multiplied it by a learned table to get a vector, and added a learned vector for the patch's position, so the model knows where each piece came from601.

An encoder and an adapter. Many assistants don't feed raw patches to the language model. A separate image model (the encoder) reads the patches first and produces one vector per patch. A small adapter then reshapes those vectors to the size of the language model's word vectors. LLaVA, an open research model, did exactly this with a single learned matrix604. To the language model, the result looks like a stretch of extra tokens.

Resolution and cost. Apps shrink large photos before sending them, and providers count picture tokens much like the simulation. Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude assistant, says in its documentation that each 28×28-pixel block is one token for Claude, so a 1,000 × 1,000-pixel photo costs 1,296 tokens. It advises keeping important text legible rather than shrinking it too far602. In the simulation, the rule for when letters become readable (about 8 pixels tall) and the loss of detail with 32-pixel patches are illustrative, as is the price.

Sound. OpenAI's Whisper speech recogniser turns audio into a spectrogram with one column every 10 milliseconds, then a first layer halves that to one vector every 20 milliseconds. It learned from 680,000 hours of audio paired with transcripts603. The voice note in the simulation is drawn, not recorded.

The maths counting patches
In words

Count how many patches fit across the picture, rounding up because a partial patch at the edge still needs a token. Do the same down. Multiply. Add the words of your question, and one token per 20 ms of sound.

With your numbers

Across: 224 ÷ 16, rounded up = 14 patches; down: the same
14 × 14 = 196 picture tokens
Total input: 6 words + 196 picture = 202 tokens

In symbols
N = ⌈W / P⌉ × ⌈H / P⌉
Nsound = t ÷ 0.02 s

N picture tokens · W, H the picture's width and height in pixels · P the patch size in pixels · ⌈ ⌉ round up to a whole number · Nsound sound tokens · t the length of the recording in seconds. Because both W and H appear, doubling the resolution multiplies N by four.

Check yourself

A model gets one token for every 20 milliseconds of sound. How many tokens is a 10-second voice note?

10 seconds is 10,000 milliseconds, and 10,000 ÷ 20 = 500 tokens.

A model misreads the small print in your photo. What is the most likely reason?

Pictures are shrunk to save tokens. Once letters are only a few pixels tall, the detail needed to tell them apart is gone.
